03 / Location recommendation

Recommend Dindigul.
Benchmark Tuticorin.

01

Input proximity matters more than port adjacency

The plant processes fresh vegetables and fresh/frozen protein but exports a much lighter, shelf-stable finished product. Moving finished cartons 180–220 km to port is normally cheaper than hauling high-moisture ingredients to a port-side factory every day.

02

Dindigul sits inside an agricultural catchment

Tamil Nadu agriculture data records paddy, maize and horticulture in Dindigul district; official horticulture references also identify Dindigul as a carrot-growing district. Nearby Theni has a dedicated food-park strategy built around paddy, maize and horticulture.

03

Poultry sourcing is stronger inland

Dindigul/Palani is within the South Tamil Nadu poultry network and closer to the Namakkal–Erode–Karur production belt than Tuticorin.

04

Tuticorin remains the export gateway

VOC Port gives the project an established container port. Current public freight listings show Tuticorin → Apapa service options, while Colombo connectivity gives additional West Africa routing choices.

3 zones
raw protein · raw vegetables · post-kill high-care

Chicken / meat stream

Receive chilled/frozen approved meat → temper under control → cook/boil → debone/size reduce → dehydrate → verify final moisture/water activity → high-care transfer. Capture cooking liquor separately for future broth recovery.

Rice / wheat stream

Clean → wash where applicable → controlled cook/steam or extrusion/instantization → dehydrate → condition. Because grain dominates the formula, line selection should be based on rehydration time and eating quality, not only throughput.

Vegetable stream

Wash → inspect → cut → product-specific blanch/pretreat → dewater → low-temperature staged dehydration → cool → grade. Mushroom should be validated independently because yield and browning control are critical.

Packing stream

Recipe-controlled blending → checkweigh → 250 g pouch fill → insert spice sachet → seal → metal detect → code → carton → palletize. Nitrogen flush or high-barrier laminate may be required after shelf-life validation.

08 / Facility & seasonal storage

1,000 m² can launch.
1,300–1,500 m² is the better plant.

The original 1,000 m² plan is a lean flow-through factory: receive, cook/dehydrate, pack, QA-release and dispatch. It has almost no strategic inventory space. Once the business intentionally buys vegetables when supply is highest and prices are lowest, the plant should dehydrate those vegetables promptly and store the dried ingredients—not hold months of fresh produce in refrigerated rooms.

Six-month vegetable inventory

Store ~16.2 t theoretical dry product, design for ~19–20 t.

At 104,000 packs/month, the base recipe uses about 2.704 t/month of finished dehydrated vegetables. Six months is ~16.2 t; adding 15–20% for quarantine lots, pallet inefficiency, held stock and operating safety brings the practical design inventory toward ~19–20 t.

Why not store it fresh?

Six months can approach ~120 t of fresh vegetable input.

Fresh mushrooms, carrots, corn, peas and scallions do not share one optimum storage regime, and several are unsuitable for months of normal cold-room holding. Buying in surplus season and dehydrating promptly avoids a large multi-temperature cold-store project and reduces spoilage exposure.

Peak-supply purchase→Receive & grade→Wash / cut / blanch→Dehydrate immediately→Moisture + aw release→Bulk high-barrier pack→Dry seasonal store→Blend / retail pack as required
Dispatch policy: finished product should not move literally from the packing line straight to the port. Keep a controlled QA-release and pallet-staging buffer so microbiology/moisture/water-activity checks, coding, documentation and container scheduling can be completed before dispatch.

Working-capital logic

Seasonal buying is not “free savings.”

Six months of the modeled fresh vegetable requirement is roughly ₹19 million at current benchmark prices; at a hypothetical 25% harvest-season discount it is still about ₹14.2 million paid much earlier. The correct decision rule is seasonal price saving − storage cost − inventory finance − shrink/loss − incremental dehydration energy. The optimum may be six months for carrots but much shorter for mushrooms or another ingredient.

Procurement rule: select dryers on guaranteed kg of water removed/hour, energy consumption per kg of water removed, final moisture/aw, cleanability and trials using the actual recipe. Seasonal procurement makes peak drying capacity more important than average annual throughput.

13 / UAE free-zone question

Useful for trading.
Not required for shipping.

01

India entity only

Best starting structure if the Indian company owns the factory, contracts ECOWAS buyers and exports directly. Lowest administration and no unnecessary UAE layer.

02

Add UAE later

Useful if buyers prefer UAE contracts/banking, you want centralized regional trading, GCC sales, investor participation, multi-country procurement or re-export warehousing.

03

Avoid physical re-export without reason

Routing India cargo through Jebel Ali before West Africa adds handling, time and freight unless you are consolidating, relabeling, financing or serving UAE/GCC customers.

QuestionAnswer
Can India ship directly to ECOWAS?Yes. Current carrier networks serve West Africa directly from western India and via transshipment options from southern India.
Does a UAE free-zone company reduce Indian export licensing?No. The Indian manufacturer/exporter still needs its Indian compliance and export documentation.
Can a UAE free-zone company re-export?Yes. UAE official guidance confirms free-zone businesses can export/re-export internationally and goods kept in the free zone for re-export generally do not attract UAE mainland customs duty.
Recommended kickoff structureIndian operating/export company first. Add UAE only when a commercial need is proven.

16 / Product 2 — jelly broth

Use the cooking liquor.
Do not introduce a new religious-risk ingredient.

Do not include broth revenue in the kickoff investment case. Capture and measure chicken/buffalo cooking liquor from day one, then commercialize 6–9 months after the instant-food line reaches stable capacity and the broth process has validated shelf life, microbiology and destination acceptance.

Halal-traceable meat batch→Cooking liquor capture→Coarse filtration→Fat separation→Fine filtration→Vacuum concentration→Season / standardize→Gel / paste fill→Retort or validated preservation
Launch timing
6–9 mo
after stable optimal operation, not after plant opening
Phase-2 CAPEX reserve
concentrator, filtration, holding, filling and preservation depending final format
Gel rule
No porcine gelatin
prefer natural collagen behavior or a validated plant-derived gelling system when texture requires assistance
Preferred formulation path

Natural broth body first; plant-derived gelling second.

Evaluate whether concentration and natural collagen deliver the required set. If additional gel strength is needed, screen food-grade agar, carrageenan, pectin or suitable starch systems for sensory performance, process stability and target-market acceptance. Every processing aid must still be checked against Halal requirements.

Traceability

Broth must inherit the meat batch identity.

Halal slaughter/source record → incoming protein lot → cooking batch → captured liquor → concentration batch → fill/retort lot. Chicken and buffalo products should remain clearly identifiable through the entire chain.

Food-safety warning: a high-moisture meat broth gel is microbiologically sensitive. For ambient ECOWAS distribution, use a validated retort/equivalent preservation process or choose a lower-water-activity concentrated paste/powder route. A chilled gel creates a cold-chain burden and should not be the default export format.

18 / Implementation roadmap

Release capital against evidence.

01

Recipe, yield & Halal review

Pilot chicken-rice, buffalo-rice and one vegetarian/alternative variant. Measure fresh/raw input, cook loss, drying recovery, energy, final moisture, aw and rehydration. Screen every ingredient and processing aid for Halal suitability.

02

Seasonal supplier calendar

Secure 3–5 suppliers for grain, poultry/buffalo, mushroom and each vegetable. Record monthly availability, historical low/high pricing, MOQ, transport and maximum practical storage horizon by ingredient.

03

Buyer + certification validation

Send samples to Nigeria, Ghana and one Francophone ECOWAS market. Confirm target landed price, pack format, shelf life, food registration and accepted Halal-certification evidence before final label design.
